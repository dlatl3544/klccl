The Reformed Churches 16–17세기

Confessions and catechisms publicly adopted by the Reformed churches through their synods and assemblies after the Reformation. These are not the work of a single author but documents the church has confessed together.

The First Helvetic Confession
The confession in 28 articles drawn up at Basel in 1536 by Swiss Reformed divines (Bullinger, Grynaeus, Myconius, and others), translated from the Latin text in Schaff's edition, with Leo Jud's German version (in High German) set beside it in the original column.		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)

The Helvetic Consensus Formula
The consensus formula of the Swiss Reformed churches drawn up in 1675 by Heidegger, Turretin, and Gerler against the teaching of the school of Saumur: the preface and 26 canons, read alongside the Latin text in Müller's edition (1903).		Müller, Die Bekenntnisschriften der reformierten Kirche (1903)

The Scots Confession
The confession drawn up in 1560 by John Knox and others and approved by the Scottish Parliament: title, preface, and 25 articles, read alongside the original Scots text in Schaff's edition (a reprint of Dunlop).		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)

The Second Helvetic Confession
The confession in 30 chapters written by Heinrich Bullinger and adopted by the Swiss Reformed churches in 1566, with the preface, the imperial edict, and the Creed of Damasus, read paragraph by paragraph alongside the Latin text in Schaff's edition.		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)

The Belgic Confession
The confession of faith in 37 articles written in French by Guido de Brès in 1561 for the churches of Flanders and the Netherlands and revised by the Synod of Dort in 1619, read article by article alongside the French text in Schaff's edition (the authentic manuscript of 1580 with the Dort revision).		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)

The Canons of Dort
The judgment of the National Synod of Dordrecht (1618–1619) on the five disputed heads of doctrine: the preface, the articles and rejections of errors of each head, and the conclusion, read article by article alongside the official Latin text in Schaff's edition.		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)

The Heidelberg Catechism
The catechism of 129 questions published at Heidelberg in 1563 by order of Elector Frederick III of the Palatinate, read question by question alongside the German text of 1563 in Schaff's edition.		Schaff, The Creeds of Christendom, vol. 3 (4th ed., 1919)
